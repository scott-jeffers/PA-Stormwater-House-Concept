Likely exempt from the township's stormwater site plan and peak-rate rules

Bedminster's 2020 ordinance (No. 239, §106) exempts small projects on large lots. On a lot over 5 acres, up to 7,500 sf of new impervious surface is exempt, as long as no more than 5,000 sf of it sits within 200 ft of the downhill property line. The new house, garage and deck add about 3,400 sf, and the closest of it is about 190 ft from the downhill line. The one catch is the gravel driveway (about 4,800 sf). If it was there before October 19, 2020, it doesn't count. If it's new, there's a cheap fix (see below).

Why the location works

  • The ground falls south toward the road at about 7%, so the road frontage is the downslope property line.
  • The closest new roof or deck is about 190 ft from that line. Table 106.2 allows up to 5,000 sf within 200 ft and 4,000 sf within 100 ft. All 3,381 sf sits between 100 and 200 ft from the line.
  • Driveway access is excluded from the setback test, but it does count toward the total unless it predates the ordinance.
  • The septic beds are east of the house on the same contour. Keep roof water and any infiltration away from them (25 ft or more is a good rule).
  • An existing culvert carries the driveway over the road ditch. Make sure it's clear and that new runoff doesn't get funneled down the driveway into the road.

The township can still deny an exemption where drainage problems are known downstream. Ask the township engineer about that when you call (step 2 in the checklist).

The path to a permit

Four moves, in order. Most of this can be done without hiring anyone.

Pin down the driveway date

Built before Oct 19, 2020? Then it doesn't count, and new impervious is about 3,400 sf, which is well within the exemption.

Confirm with the township

Call the township engineer with the numbers on this page. Confirm the §106 exemption, how gravel and decks are treated, and what they want submitted.

File the exemption + E&S sketch

Application form ×3, plot plan ×3, fee/escrow. Add a simple erosion control plan, and the Conservation District letter if the township asks for it.

Build it right

Silt sock, a stone entrance, and stockpiles away from the road. Send downspouts to lawn or a rain garden. Seed and mulch fast.

What goes in the E&S plan

A one-sheet sketch on the site plan plus a short narrative. These are the standard practices for a single house.

Before digging

  • Call PA One Call (811) at least 3 business days ahead
  • Stake the limit of disturbance
  • Stone construction entrance where the driveway meets the work area, to keep mud off the road
  • Compost filter sock (12–18") along the downhill edge of the work area, following the contour

During construction

  • Strip topsoil and stockpile it uphill, surrounded by sock
  • Keep stockpiles out of the drive, ditch and culvert
  • Inspect after every rain and repair the sock
  • Bring in the septic bed and trenches last, and backfill fast

Finishing

  • Re-spread topsoil, then seed and mulch within days of final grade
  • Use erosion blanket on any slope steeper than 3:1
  • Remove the sock once there's 70% or more grass cover
  • Narrative notes: sequence, contact person, inspection log

Good drainage practice (cheap, and worth doing anyway)

Around the house

  • Grade at least 6" of fall in the first 10 ft away from the foundation
  • Run downspouts out at least 10 ft onto lawn, a splash block, or a small rain garden downhill
  • Put a footing drain to daylight downhill, never toward the septic
  • Keep the deck open-slatted over a stone bed, so rain drips through and soaks in

Protect the septic, well and road

  • No roof water, dry wells or rain gardens within 25 ft of the septic beds or well
  • Don't route runoff down the driveway into the road. Use a broad dip or a stone diversion to turn it onto grass
  • Keep the driveway culvert at the road clear, and protect the inlet and outlet with stone
  • Leave the existing meadow and woods undisturbed. They do the stormwater work for free

If the exemption doesn't apply

The likely fixes, cheapest first. None of them involves a detention pond.

1 · Infiltrating deck

The ordinance says decks aren't counted when they're built as a BMP that promotes infiltration. Gapped boards with no roof over a stone bed should qualify. That drops scenario B from 8,194 to 7,182 sf, under the 7,500 limit.

2 · Narrower driveway

The driveway averages about 14.6 ft wide. Going to 12 ft saves roughly 850 sf. Ask the township whether an open-graded gravel drive could also get a pervious credit (Appendix J allows credits on slopes of 1–5%).

3 · Simplified-method plan

Capture the first 2 inches of runoff from new impervious areas in dry wells, trenches or rain gardens (Ord. 239 §303, method CG-2, which skips peak-rate control). A one-sheet design that a PE stamps.

Rough sizing calculator (2-inch capture)

For early planning only. A final design must be confirmed by the township engineer or a PE.

How it works: volume = area × 2 in ÷ 12. A stone bed holds about 40% water, so its gross volume is volume ÷ 0.4. A rain garden's area is volume ÷ ponding depth. Infiltration facilities must drain within 72 hours, have at least 18 inches over rock or a high water table, and sit well away from the foundation, septic and well.
